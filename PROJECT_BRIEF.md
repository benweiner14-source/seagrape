## What This Is

Seagrape Supply Co. is a bespoke lifestyle apparel studio designing hyper-personalized capsule collections for private country clubs. The business fills a genuine white space: every CC pro shop sells the same performance polos and quarter zips. Nobody sells lifestyle apparel — the thing a member actually wants to wear to the airport, on a boat, or to dinner after a round.

This is not a vendor relationship. It's a creative studio that designs a small seasonal capsule (4-6 pieces) specific to each club's identity, history, and culture. The product is a Comfort Colors heavyweight tee with original illustrated graphics that tell the story of that specific club.

The pilot client is **Admirals Cove Golf & Yacht Club** in Jupiter, Florida.

## The Brand

**Name:** Seagrape Supply Co.

**Why this name:** The seagrape tree (Coccoloba uvifera) is native to South Florida's coastline — gnarled, low-spreading, with round waxy leaves and sparse fruit clusters. It marks the transition from land to shore. The name is hyper-local, completely ownable in the apparel space (confirmed clean — no meaningful US competitor), and carries an old Florida trading post energy that fits the brand perfectly.

**Brand energy:** Old Florida trading post. Utilitarian, worn, not precious. Think: a shop that's been on the Intracoastal since 1952. Not preppy, not streetwear — somewhere between the two. Comfort Colors blanks, original illustrated graphics, hang tags that mean something.

**Comparable brands:** Malbon Golf, Lafarve, Kith. The name shouldn't explain the product. The brand builds a world; golf and boating are the vehicles.

**Brand positioning:** Not a vendor. A creative studio that happens to sell shirts. Each capsule is a limited creative project about a specific club. Members aren't buying merch — they're buying a piece of their club's identity that was made specifically for them.

## The Business Model

**Sales motion:** Approach club GMs or head pros. Present a full bespoke capsule concept — lookbook, mockups, the story — before a single shirt is printed.

**Pricing structure:**
- Design fee: $3,000–$5,000 upfront (covers creative, AI generation, lookbook build)
- Pre-order model: members pre-order through a private URL — only print what's sold, zero inventory risk for the club
- Retail price: $55–$65 per tee
- Your cost per unit: ~$14–16 (blank $8–10 + screen print $4–6)
- Your margin per unit: ~$39–49 at pre-order retail

**What a successful capsule looks like:**
- Admirals Cove has ~500–600 member families
- Conservative 10% buy rate = 55–60 units
- Design fee ($4K) + unit margin (55 × $40 = $2,200) = ~$6,200 per capsule
- 3 drops per club per year = ~$18K per club
- 5 clubs = ~$90K lean operation

**The unfair advantage:** AI design means a full capsule concept can be pitched within days of first contact. Traditional apparel vendors take weeks and charge for design time. Speed is the pitch.

## Pilot Client: Admirals Cove Golf & Yacht Club

**Location:** Jupiter, Florida
**Founded:** 1987
**Built on:** John D. MacArthur's land — a billionaire who dug miles of canals without government permission in the 1950s–60s. This is a real founding myth worth putting on a shirt.
**Designation:** Platinum Clubs of the World
**Golf:** Two courses designed by Robert von Hagge. US Open Qualifier host for over a decade.
**Boating:** Equal to golf in club identity. 63 deep water slips, vessels up to 165 feet, 5 miles of navigable internal waterways, yacht club founded 1990.
**The lighthouse:** Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse, built 1860, reddish-brown brick, 105 feet tall. During WWII it was a Navy radio station that helped sink 60+ German submarines. Sits where the Loxahatchee River meets the Intracoastal meets the Atlantic.
**The waterway:** ICW runs directly past the club. Channel markers 49 (green) and 52 (red) are landmarks every member who boats knows by heart.

## The Capsule — Five Drops

### Drop 1 — "The Inlet" (Lighthouse from the Water)
**Status:** Prompt refined, not yet generated to satisfaction. Earlier versions were too generic (symmetrical lighthouse flanked by palms). Direction shifted to view from the water looking back.

**Blank:** Deep teal Comfort Colors 1717 (match to Blue Spruce or Dark Teal colorway)
**Colors:** Cream illustration, brick red on lighthouse tower only, teal = shirt color (transparent background)
**Placement:** Full back graphic

**Generation prompt:**
> "Vintage woodcut illustration showing the view from the water looking back toward Jupiter Inlet — the perspective of a boater who has just passed through the inlet channel. The Jupiter lighthouse stands on the bluff to the right, reddish-brown brick, seen from a low water-level angle. Composition in three layers: marsh grass and coastal scrub in the immediate foreground, the Loxahatchee River across the middle ground with subtle water texture and reflection, dense Florida tree line along the far bank with the reddish-brown Jupiter lighthouse rising above the canopy — tall, narrow, slightly off-center to the right. Asymmetric natural composition. The lighthouse emerges from the treeline, not standing alone. Woodcut style, hand-drawn ink lines, confident and slightly worn. Three colors — deep teal background, cream illustration, brick red on the lighthouse tower only. The foreground marsh grass adds depth and frames the scene from below. Bottom text: 'ADMIRALS COVE — JUPITER INLET' in small condensed serif, 'EST. 1987' beneath. No border, no frame. Feels like an illustration from a 1960s boating or fishing magazine. Print-ready for back of a deep teal Comfort Colors heavyweight tee."

**Key issue to avoid:** Do not generate a symmetrical lighthouse flanked by two palms — that's a gift shop tee. The lighthouse must emerge from a treeline, seen from water level, off-center.
